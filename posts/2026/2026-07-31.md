## There are reasons

I didn’t necessarily want to. I’ve had my beloved iPhone 13 Mini for a while. I purchased it from the Apple Certified Refurbished store 3 or 4 years ago and have been very happy. But there are a few reasons why I felt like it was time for an upgrade.

1. **My phone struggles with heat**. The small volume means the body heats up quickly, especially when charging in my car. On numerous occasions when driving with the windows down and AC off, my phone will stop charging because it has become too hot. 
2. **My phone cannot do three things at once**. I cycle often. When I do, my phone (through my fitness watch) is tracking a workout. Because I am cycling, the phone is also connected to my little Beeline Velo 2 on my bike to provide turn-by-turn directions for the route. These two things seem to consume all system resources. If I try to take out my phone for a photo, one of two things will happen:
    - the phone will lock up or lag badly trying to open the camera app; or
    - the bluetooth connection to my Velo 2 will drop.
3. **Battery life is not awesome**. It was definitely better than I feared when I first purchased the phone, but it’s not the greatest. I do need to be careful with how I spend the battery power during a day. The phone never died on me, but I also didn’t push it much either. And a charger was always handy at home, at work, or in the car.

In addition to these issues with the phone itself, there are external factors that motivated me:

1. **The RAM Shortage**. This has made electronics prices skyrocket. Apple already raised prices on MacBooks. It is only a matter of time before iPhone prices follow. I wanted to make a purchase before the price increase that is likely to come with the new version of the phone.
2. **Siri AI**. I think Apple might finally provide real on-phone LLM-based assistance with the updated Siri. I knew the original announcement of Apple Intelligence was marketing garbage and I was right. Nothing came of it. However, I think that, this time, they may have figured a few things out. I think now is the time to get a phone that will support the new features. The iPhone Air and iPhone 17 Pro are the only two phones that will support the most advanced features.
3. **FOMO**. I’ll admit it. I’m a sucker for shiny things.

## Choose, but choose wisely

Let’s save the suspense. I’ve upgraded from the iPhone 13 Mini to the iPhone 17 Pro. This is the first time I’ve ever purchased the Pro version of an iPhone and the first time since the iPhone X that I have gotten a phone this expensive. This phone is overkill. But there were a number of factors that led me to choosing this phone over the other options available to me.

### No mini no mo’

Apple won’t make a small phone even though they clearly now have the technology available to make a really good one. Think about what a great phone an iPhone 17 Mini would be. It wouldn’t suffer the same battery limitations because the battery tech is better and the processor is more efficient. But they refuse. Android phones aren’t any better. They’re all huge so they can fit a crazy large battery that will support unoptimized or inefficient hardware and software and so they can post an ever-larger number on the spec sheet. So upgrading means dealing with a much larger phone. And for the past several generations of iPhone the size hasn’t really changed much. And compared to their series 17 counterparts, the iPhones 15 and 16 – which are readily available refurbished – are less capable phones at the same screen size.

### 17 was a really good year

The 17 series of phones was a significant update over the previous years with excellent base model phones, the addition of the iPhone Air experiment, and the revised design for the Pro-model phones. These phones have improved displays, improved selfie cameras, and more efficient processors. Purchasing a newer phone also means I will (likely) get more life out of the phone. I got 4 years of use out of a phone that I purchased refurbished – meaning it was already a year out of date when I bought it. So a phone from this model year (even if the model year is nearing the end) should give me a long service life.

### Secondary market value

I didn’t want to pay more than $1000 for a new phone. So the first thing I did was start searching eBay – researching prices and watching auctions. I was surprised to discover that the iPhone 17 and iPhone 17 Pro are holding their value exceptionally well. I’ve seen several posts asking good money for iPhone 17s with *broken glass*. Now, I understand it’s been less than a year since the phones came out, but consider the fate of the iPhone Air. It came out around the same time but is routinely available for $250 less than retail price. That’s a huge drop-off. I considered getting a used iPhone Air to take advantage of that discount, but that blade has two edges. The phone will likely continue to drop in price on the secondary market, making it worth less if I decided to resell. The iPhone 17 Pro, on the other hand, will still be valuable in a couple of years, particularly if RAM prices continue to drive up the cost of new devices.

## Houston, we have an eSim

Ultimately, I was convinced to get a new phone on a trip to the local Apple Store. Because the price of used phones was so close to retail, I didn’t see much advantage to buying on the secondary market. The minimal price difference between used on eBay and new in the store is about the cost of piece of mind. I’m getting a clean phone unburdened by someone else’s problems.

The one thing I didn’t consider was the SIM. My iPhone 13 Mini has a physical SIM, something that disappeared from US-model phones starting a year later with the iPhone 14 (I think). To make matters worse, my phone provider (I will not name names) did not support eSIM. How this could be a thing in 2026 I’m not sure. The last time I traveled abroad, I was able to purchase an eSIM for my phone for a month so that I would have local phone and data service and it worked great.

I had planned to trade in my iPhone 13 Mini a the time I purchased my new phone. However, because of the eSIM issue, I ended up needing to keep my phone to use to hook up service to my new phone. It turned out to also be useful in making sure that I had transferred all important data – especially passwords, passkeys, and authenticator tokens – to the new phone. And Apple, to their credit, seems to know this sort of thing can happen. They gave me 14 days to bring back my old phone to trade in and have the money apply to the purchase of the new phone. Very convenient.

## How it was / How it’s going

I have become comfortable with the size of the phone. That was probably inevitable. You become used to what you have, especially when using it every day. The weight is another matter. The phone is much heavier than my old Mini and it is still noticeable.

I did not like the always-on display. It works for the Apple Watch, but for whatever reason I cannot get used to it on the phone. It also unlocks too easily when the display is always on. It has happened more than once that my phone has been unlocked and active in my pocket because the phone unlocked too quickly after I locked the phone. Lift to wake works perfectly fine. I don’t need the display to always be on. I might feel different if I did not have an Apple Watch for viewing notifications, but I do.

I like the camera button, I think. The jury is still out on that. At least, with a case on, accidental activation of the camera has not been an issue.

People make a huge fuss about the high refresh rate on modern screens. They swear it makes a big difference and "can’t go back" once they’ve had a phone with a 120Hz refresh rate. This is garbage. I see no difference at all. Don’t get me wrong; the screen on the iPhone 17 pro is amazing.

The oleophobic coatings they put on screens now are outstanding. This alone might be the biggest screen technology advancement in the past few phone generations. Screw refresh rate. Give me a screen that isn’t covered by finger oil smudges. That is a quality of life improvement I can get behind. and it doesn’t cost me any battery life.
